The reform is often summed up as "electronic invoicing." In reality, it rests on **two complementary obligations**: e-invoicing and e-reporting. Confusing the two risks leaving some of your flows outside the scope of compliance.

## E-invoicing: invoices between businesses

E-invoicing covers the **issuing and receiving of electronic invoices** between French, VAT-registered companies (B2B), as well as with the public sector (B2G).

In practice:

- the invoice is issued in a structured format (Factur-X, UBL or CII);
- it passes through a **Certified Platform (PDP)**;
- its data is transmitted to the tax authorities.

## E-reporting: transactions outside electronic invoicing

E-reporting is the **essential counterpart** to e-invoicing. It covers transactions that don't go through an electronic invoice:

- sales to individuals (**B2C**), in France or abroad;
- transactions with foreign companies (intra-EU sales and purchases, exports);
- certain services and transactions under the VAT reverse-charge mechanism.

Here, what's transmitted to the tax authorities is a **data extract** (amount excl./incl. VAT, date, customer's country, VAT rate, payment method...), not a single structured file.

> Worth remembering: if you sell to individuals or internationally, e-reporting applies to you — even without an electronic invoice.

## One timeline, the same need to prepare ahead

Both obligations follow the **same timeline** (2026-2027) and go through the same Certified Platforms. Mapping all your flows — B2B, B2C, domestic and international — is therefore an essential first step.
