The e-invoicing reform is too often seen as a simple box to tick. That's a mistake of perspective. Behind compliance lies a **deep transformation of the finance function** — and, for those who anticipate it, a genuine competitive edge.

## From data you endure to data you control

Today, accounting production relies on heterogeneous flows: paper, emails, PDFs, professional portals. This diversity generates re-keying, errors, and considerable lost time.

With the reform, data arrives **structured and consistent**:

- a standardised entry point for every invoice;
- the elimination of re-keying errors;
- full traceability, from issuance to payment.

## Toward automated accounting production

Structured data paves the way for **largely automated accounting production** and, eventually, pre-filled VAT returns.

> Worth remembering: value no longer lies in data entry, but in managing flows, data reliability, and the advisory that follows from it.

## Time given back to steering and advisory

By automating low-value tasks, the reform frees up time for what really matters: **analysing, steering, advising**. The business owner gains a real-time read on their activity; the accountant refocuses on their role as a strategic partner.

## Act rather than endure

Companies that see the reform as a pure cost will suffer the deadline. Those who approach it as a transformation project will gain a head start. The difference is being made **right now**, in how you anticipate it.
